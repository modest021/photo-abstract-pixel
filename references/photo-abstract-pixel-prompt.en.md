# Photo Abstract Pixel — English prompt

Semantically redraw my uploaded image as one complete, restrained abstract pixel artwork. Treat the source image as the only content source: preserve its subjects, composition, directions, layers, negative space, and overall mood while deliberately discarding photorealistic detail. The result must look authored as pixel art, never like a downsample, enlarged mosaic, or filter.

Internally identify three to six decisive spatial and color relationships. Choose a logical low-resolution grid that fits the scene. Use one primary abstraction grammar—large color fields, stepped contours, or grid rhythm—with at most two supporting grammars. Establish the area, position, proportion, and direction of subjects and background first, then use sparse small pixel clusters for the minimum identity cues needed.

Derive a compact palette of 6–14 colors from the source, merging nearby hues and value levels. Use highly saturated accents only when they already exist in the image, and do not default to pure black for shadows. Use explicit square pixels, hard edges, and coherent clusters. No antialiasing, soft focus, smooth gradients, photographic texture, random speckling, meaningless dithering, glitch effects, uniform mosaic tiles, or mechanical outlines.

Use medium abstraction by default so the subjects and scene relationships remain recognizable. Preserve pose, hairstyle, or signature accessories for people; body shape, ears, or marking rhythm for animals; silhouette and one to three identity features for architecture; and horizon, major layers, and light/color relationships for landscapes.

Keep the original aspect ratio by default and return only one finished image. Unless I explicitly request otherwise, add no new subjects, props, text, borders, poster panels, game UI, decorative icons, watermark, chibi proportions, dot eyes, blush, retro HUD, or neon cyberpunk elements. A slight crop is allowed, but never change the core event or subject relationships.
